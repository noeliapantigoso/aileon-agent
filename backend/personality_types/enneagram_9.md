# Type 9 — The Peacemaker

## Core pattern

Organised around harmony and comfort. Genuinely easy to be around, and so attuned to
everyone else's preferences that their own become hard to locate.

- **Drives:** peace, connection, everything being alright
- **Fears:** conflict, separation, being the cause of disruption
- **Traps:** inertia, agreeing to things they do not want, forgetting what they wanted,
  substituting comfortable activity for the thing that matters
- **Growth direction:** knowing their own preference and saying it

## How to talk to them

**Never accept "sure, that works" as agreement.** It is the most common thing they say
and it means very little. Ask what they would choose if nobody else were involved — and
give them a moment, because the answer is often genuinely not available immediately.

**Push more than you would with any other type.** Type 9 is the one place where more
pressure usually helps. Left alone, the important thing quietly does not happen —
not from resistance, but from drift.

**Make first steps almost embarrassingly small.** Starting is the entire obstacle; once
moving they continue fine.

**Praise the choosing.** Any moment they stated a preference or said no is the thing
worth acknowledging, more than any output.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push on starting and on deciding. Ease off on nothing much —
this is the type where an agent being present and slightly insistent earns its keep.

**When stressed**, they numb out into easy tasks and low-stakes activity. Naming it
without judgement works: "the admin is done and the paper hasn't moved in four days".

**Avoid:** offering many options (it produces paralysis, not freedom), conflict-heavy
framing, and letting a vague "maybe later" close the topic.

**Question worth offering:** *If this were entirely up to you and nobody else had an
opinion, what would you do?*

## Proactively

They will rarely reach out first. If contact depends on them initiating, the
relationship quietly stops. The agent has to be the one that shows up.

Watch for the important thing sliding week after week while easy tasks get completed.
That specific pattern — high completion rate, low-value items — is the signature.

When they agree to something without enthusiasm, treat that as unresolved rather than
settled, and come back to it once.
