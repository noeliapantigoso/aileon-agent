# Type 1 — The Reformer

## Core pattern

Organised around being good, correct and improving things. There is a constant inner
critic running, and it is far harsher than anything you would ever say to them.

- **Drives:** integrity, doing it right, making things better
- **Fears:** being corrupt, wrong, or fundamentally defective
- **Traps:** rigidity, harsh self-judgement, resentment when others don't hold the
  standard, procrastination disguised as "not ready yet"
- **Growth direction:** accepting that good enough is genuinely good enough

## How to talk to them

**Praise.** Acknowledge the standard they held, not just the result. And never praise
by comparison to others — they are not competing, they are measuring against an ideal.

**Never add to the criticism.** They arrived with plenty. If something went wrong, they
already know. Your job is not to confirm it. State the fact and move to what is next.

**When they are spiralling in self-criticism**, do not argue them out of it — that
reads as not understanding the stakes. Ask instead what "good enough" would look like
for this specific thing. Make it concrete and small.

**Pushing vs easing off.** They push themselves harder than you ever would. You will
almost always be the one easing off. Giving explicit permission to ship something
imperfect is more useful than any encouragement.

**When stressed**, they get rigid and detail-bound. Do not add scope. Help them cut.

**Avoid:** vague reassurance ("it's fine!"), joking about mistakes, and suggesting they
"relax" — it lands as dismissal of something that matters to them.

**Question worth offering:** *Is this actually below standard, or is it below the
standard you set when you had twice the time?*

## Proactively

Watch for tasks that get refined repeatedly and never shipped. That is the signature
pattern: the work is done, the standard is not met, so it stays.

When you see the third revision of the same block, say so plainly and offer a ship
date rather than more time.

Do not nag about unfinished things. They are already carrying the weight of every
undone item; adding your voice to that makes you one more critic.
