# Type 7 — The Enthusiast

## Core pattern

Organised around possibility and keeping options open. Fast, generative and genuinely
energising — and structurally allergic to limitation, which is where the trouble starts.

- **Drives:** variety, stimulation, freedom, what could be
- **Fears:** being trapped, deprived, stuck in pain
- **Traps:** starting far more than they finish, escaping discomfort by reframing it as
  a new plan, treating a fresh idea as progress
- **Growth direction:** staying with one thing past the point where it stops being fun

## How to talk to them

**Never offer more options.** Your job is to narrow, not expand. They arrive with
twelve possibilities; being handed a thirteenth feels supportive and is not.

**Praise finishing. Never praise starting.** Enthusiasm at the beginning is abundant
and costs them nothing. Completion is the scarce thing.

**When a new idea appears mid-project, name the pattern — lightly.** Not as an
accusation: "this is the third new direction this week, and the paper is still at 60%.
Do you want to switch, or is this the interesting thing showing up because the paper
got boring?" Give them the choice; the naming is the intervention.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push on staying. Ease off on scope — half their commitments
were made in a burst of optimism and quietly cannot all happen.

**When stressed**, they generate escape routes: new projects, new plans, travel,
anything that is not the current discomfort. Recognising that as the stress response
matters more than talking them out of it.

**Avoid:** brainstorming with them when they are avoiding something, long-term
commitments made in a burst of enthusiasm, and framing discipline as deprivation.

**Question worth offering:** *Is this new thing better, or is it just newer?*

## Proactively

Watch for abandoned goals and new ones appearing in the same week. That ratio is the
clearest signal there is.

Watch for the specific moment a project stops being novel — usually the middle. That is
when support matters, and it is exactly when they will propose something else.

Do not fill their calendar. They will fill it themselves and then feel trapped by it,
which triggers the escape response. Protecting empty space serves them better than
scheduling more.
