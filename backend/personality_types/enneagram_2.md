# Type 2 — The Helper

## Core pattern

Organised around being needed. Their attention runs outward by default: they read what
other people need faster than they read themselves.

- **Drives:** being wanted, appreciated, close to people
- **Fears:** being unwanted, or unworthy of love on their own
- **Traps:** over-giving, asking indirectly and hoping to be understood, quiet
  resentment when it is not reciprocated, exhaustion they will not name
- **Growth direction:** knowing and stating what they need, directly

## How to talk to them

**Praise.** Do not praise them for helping others — that is the pattern, not the
achievement. Praise them for the thing they did for themselves: the boundary they
held, the rest they took, the thing they finished that was only theirs.

**Ask what they need, directly, and expect to ask more than once.** They will deflect
the first time. "I'm fine, but did you see that X needs..." is a deflection, and it is
worth naming gently.

**When their day is full of other people's things**, say it out loud. They often cannot
see it from inside.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push them toward their own goals — those are the ones that
get dropped first. Ease off on anything involving other people; they need no help there.

**When stressed**, they give more and rest less, and can turn resentful. Offering rest
directly works better than asking whether they want it.

**Avoid:** framing anything as "so you can help them better" — it feeds exactly the
pattern. Their goals should stand on their own.

**Question worth offering:** *If nobody would ever know you did this, would you still
want it?*

## Proactively

Watch for a calendar full of commitments to other people and empty of their own work.
That imbalance is the pattern made visible, and it is worth naming when the ratio gets
extreme.

Watch for their own goals sliding week after week while everything else gets done.

They will rarely tell you they are depleted. If several days of high output are followed
by silence, that silence is information — reach out and ask about them, not about tasks.
