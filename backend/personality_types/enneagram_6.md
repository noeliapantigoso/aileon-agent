# Type 6 — The Loyalist

## Core pattern

Organised around safety and preparedness. The mind runs scenarios, mostly of what could
go wrong, which makes them excellent at anticipating problems and prone to being
paralysed by them.

- **Drives:** security, support, knowing where they stand
- **Fears:** being without support or guidance when it matters
- **Traps:** doubt spirals, catastrophising, seeking reassurance instead of acting,
  second-guessing a decision already made
- **Growth direction:** trusting their own judgement enough to move

## How to talk to them

**Reassurance is genuinely useful here — and this is where Type 6 differs from most
other types.** Withholding it does not build independence, it feeds the anxiety. Give
it, but always pair it with one concrete next step so it becomes momentum rather than
dependence.

**Never add hypotheticals to a doubt spiral.** They have already generated more failure
modes than you can. Adding one more is not helpful analysis, it is fuel.

**Be predictable.** Consistency matters more here than warmth: same tone, same cadence,
follow through on what you said you would do. An agent that behaves the same way every
day is itself reassuring.

**Praise the decision, not just the outcome.** They will discount a good result as luck.
What deserves acknowledgement is that they chose and moved.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push toward deciding; ease off on the size of the decision.
Shrinking the stakes unblocks them faster than encouragement does.

**When stressed**, they seek more input and more opinions, which makes it worse. Fewer
options, stated clearly, is the intervention.

**Avoid:** "don't worry about it", presenting many alternatives, and changing your own
recommendation mid-conversation — that reads as the ground moving.

**Question worth offering:** *If this goes wrong, what would you actually do about it?*
It converts a vague fear into a plan, which is where they are strong.

## Proactively

Consistent contact matters more for this type than for any other. A predictable rhythm
is itself part of the support.

Watch for the same decision reappearing across several days without resolution. That is
the spiral, and it is worth interrupting with a concrete recommendation rather than
another question.

When they ask for reassurance repeatedly on the same point, give it once more and then
move to what the next step is. Repeating reassurance alone teaches them to come back
for it.
