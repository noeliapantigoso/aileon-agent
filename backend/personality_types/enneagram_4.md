# Type 4 — The Individualist

## Core pattern

Organised around authenticity and significance. Feelings are treated as information,
not noise, and there is a persistent sense that something essential is missing.

- **Drives:** being authentic, being deeply understood, meaning over efficiency
- **Fears:** being ordinary, having no identity of their own
- **Traps:** melancholy that becomes a place to live, envy of other people's apparent
  ease, waiting for the right feeling before starting
- **Growth direction:** acting without waiting for the mood to arrive

## How to talk to them

**Never try to cheer them up.** Optimism aimed at a Type 4 reads as not being taken
seriously, and it costs you the conversation. Take the feeling at face value first.

**Then ask what can happen anyway.** The sequence matters: acknowledge, then act. "That
sounds genuinely heavy. Is there a small piece of it that can move today regardless?"

**Praise what they finished, not how unique they are.** Being told they are different
is not encouragement — it is the identity they are already over-invested in. Completion
is the rarer achievement.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push on starting; ease off on outcomes. The blocker is
almost never capability, it is waiting for conditions to feel right.

**When stressed**, they withdraw into feeling and away from doing. The intervention is
smaller steps, not encouragement.

**Avoid:** comparisons to how others do it, "just get started" without acknowledging
the state first, and treating a mood as an obstacle to be argued with.

**Question worth offering:** *Does this have to feel right before it can begin?*

## Proactively

Watch for long gaps where a meaningful project sits untouched while smaller things get
done. That is usually waiting-for-inspiration, not lack of interest.

Watch for a pattern of starting on good days only. Their output correlating tightly
with mood is the thing worth naming — gently, as data, not as criticism.

When they go quiet, do not assume disengagement. Reaching out with something specific
and small works better than a general check-in.
