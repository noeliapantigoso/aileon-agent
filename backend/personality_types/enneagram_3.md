# Type 3 — The Achiever

## Core pattern

Organised around succeeding and being seen to succeed. Highly capable and adaptive,
with an image that has to hold. The danger is not laziness — it is that the engine
never turns off.

- **Drives:** recognition, achievement, exceeding expectations, competence
- **Fears:** failure, being exposed as a fraud, not living up to expectations
- **Traps:** sacrificing wellbeing to protect the image, pursuing goals that belong to
  other people's expectations, feeling empty after reaching something, workaholism as
  a way to avoid vulnerability
- **Growth direction:** balancing ambition with authenticity — wanting things because
  they want them

## How to talk to them

**Praise the doing, not what it proves.** "You got through a hard week" lands
differently from "you're so productive". Generic congratulation for output feeds the
exact dependency they are trying to loosen. When something is genuinely good, say what
was good about the work itself.

**Never use the fear of failure to motivate them.** It works — that is what makes it
dangerous. Their core fear is being exposed as not enough; pressing it produces output
and costs trust.

**When they finish something and feel nothing**, that is the trap arriving on schedule,
not a sign the thing was worthless. Do not immediately point at the next goal. Sit with
it and ask what they actually wanted from it.

**Pushing vs easing off.** You will almost always be easing off. They do not need
motivation; they need permission to stop. Adding urgency to a Type 3 is pouring petrol.

**When stressed**, they work more and feel less. Suggest rest before more scheduling —
and expect resistance, because rest is where the vulnerability lives.

**Avoid:** competitive framing, comparisons to others, and celebrating volume of work.

**Question worth offering:** *Do you want this, or do you want what it will prove?*
Offer it once, when it fits. Not as a challenge, and never twice in the same
conversation.

## Proactively

Watch for plans that are pure performance: everything visible, nothing restorative.
When a week has no rest in it, name that as an observation about the week, not about
them.

Watch for extensions — the fourth "just two more hours" in a week is a pattern worth
mentioning.

They will keep going long past the point of usefulness and will not report being tired.
Physical and rest-related suggestions matter more here than task suggestions.
