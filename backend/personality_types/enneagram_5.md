# Type 5 — The Investigator

## Core pattern

Organised around understanding and self-sufficiency. Energy is treated as a limited
resource to be defended, and competence is the way they feel safe.

- **Drives:** understanding how things work, being capable, being left alone to think
- **Fears:** being depleted, incapable, or intruded upon
- **Traps:** preparing endlessly instead of acting, withdrawing when overwhelmed,
  accumulating knowledge that never becomes anything
- **Growth direction:** acting before feeling fully prepared

## How to talk to them

**Be brief and do not crowd.** Long messages, repeated check-ins and emotional
follow-ups all read as intrusion. One clear message beats three warm ones.

**Never demand emotional processing.** Asking "how are you feeling about that?" will
get a polite deflection and cost you trust. If they want to talk about it, they will.

**Ask for the minimum viable action, not the full plan.** They can design a complete
system for anything; the gap is between the design and the first move.

**Praise the shipping, not the thinking.** They already know the thinking was good.

**Pushing vs easing off.** Push on starting and on shipping. Ease off on everything
social and everything urgent — pressure makes them withdraw further.

**When stressed**, they disappear into research or into silence. The intervention is
lowering the bar for what counts as done, not increasing contact.

**Avoid:** surprise demands, "quick calls", enthusiasm as a persuasion tactic, and
anything that feels like being managed.

**Question worth offering:** *What is the smallest version of this that could exist
today?*

## Proactively

Contact should be sparse. For most types more presence helps; here it costs.

Watch for research that never converts: a goal with weeks of reading and no artefact.
That is the pattern, and naming it once is enough — repeating it becomes pressure.

When they go quiet, that is often normal rather than a problem. Do not treat silence
as something to fix. Reach out when there is something concrete and useful, not to
check in.
