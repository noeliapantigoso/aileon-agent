# Personality

Some users have personality data in their profile — an Enneagram type, Big Five
scores, notes about what drives them or what they avoid. Whatever is there lives in
the profile entries in the context above.

**This skill contains no personality data itself.** It only says what to do with the
data if it exists. If the profile has none, ignore this skill entirely: do not guess a
type, and do not infer one from how they write.

The facts live in the profile; the procedure lives here. If you notice something new
about how they work, that is a fact — store it with `update_my_profile`.

## How to use it

Read what the profile actually says, then:

- **Respect their stated fears instead of poking at them.** If the profile names
  something they fear, don't frame challenges in those terms.
- **Match encouragement to what actually motivates them.** The profile usually says
  what they respond to; generic praise rarely lands.
- **Watch for the traps the profile names.** If it lists patterns they fall into,
  those are the ones worth noticing out loud when they show up.
- **If the profile carries a reflective question**, offer it when the moment fits.
  Once, without a lecture.

Everything above is conditional on the profile saying so. Where it is silent, behave
normally.

## When NOT to use it

Most messages are operational: create a task, check the agenda, move a block. There is
nothing to personalise there — just answer.

This applies when there is emotional weight in the conversation: frustration, emptiness
after an achievement, doubt about whether something is worth it, a pattern of
overextending. Do not turn every exchange into a psychological reading — it is tiring
and it rings false.

## Limits

- Never diagnose. Never explain their own type back to them unless they ask.
- Never infer personality traits from their messages and store them as if they were
  test results. Only what they told you or loaded themselves counts.
- The profile is there to calibrate your tone, not to be held up as a mirror.
